# Level 1 Pilot Financial Model

## Purpose
Model initial investment, operating costs, cost per student, tuition, break-even, cash flow, and pilot economics.

## Separate financial categories
### Initial investment
Laboratory, workstations, tools, electronics, robotics/mechanical equipment, safety, technology, storage, and setup.

### Fixed monthly costs
Rent, electricity, water, internet, salaries, administration, accounting, software, insurance where applicable, cleaning, maintenance, marketing, and logistics.

### Variable student costs
Components, consumables, printed material, workbook, certificate, damage allowance, kit components, and other student-specific costs.

## Teacher cost
Calculate the true cost of a 90-minute class including teaching, preparation, setup, cleanup, documentation, assessment, parent communication, and administrative time.

## Course cost
For the 24-session course calculate:
teacher/assistant + components + consumables + printing + certificates + equipment replacement + laboratory overhead + marketing + administration.

Calculate cost per student for 4, 6, 8, 10, and 12 students.

## Revenue
Model tuition by student, contribution per student, course revenue, and payment timing.

## Break-even
Calculate:
- break-even students
- break-even revenue
- minimum viable class
- lab utilization
- maximum economic capacity

## Capacity
Model students/workstation, workstations/lab, students/class, classes/day/week, operating days/month, and annual capacity.

## Cash flow
Track opening cash, enrollment payments, other revenue, equipment purchases, rent, salaries, utilities, marketing, consumables, maintenance, other expenses, and closing cash.

Profit and cash must be treated separately.

## Financial scenarios
Model small pilot (4–6), normal (8–10), and full (12) enrollment.

## Pilot financial experiment
Record actual enrollment, tuition, equipment purchases, consumables, teacher hours, operating costs, attendance, cancellations, refunds, and damage. Compare actual results with the model and revise assumptions.
